# Our Place, Fürth — website research & build brief

Research date: 5 September 2026. This report separates **verified / source-reported information** from **recommendations**. Restaurant menus, hours, listings and reviews can change: confirm all operational data with the owner before publishing.

## Executive direction

**Recommendation:** Reposition the digital experience around a very specific truth already visible in the menu and customer language: _American barbecue and cocktail nights in a historic Fürth setting_. The site should make three things effortless within seconds: see the food, book a table, and find the restaurant. It should feel warm, smoky, energetic and unmistakably local—not like a generic American-diner template.

The decisive upgrade is not decorative animation. It is replacing a nearly content-free booking gateway with a credible, appetising, accessible restaurant presence that answers practical questions before guests ask them.

### Audience and differentiators — interpretation

- **Likely primary groups:** local friend groups, families, celebratory groups, casual dates, visitors staying nearby, and BBQ-seeking diners from the Nürnberg–Fürth area.
- **Distinctive assets to foreground:** hickory smoke, Black Angus / house buns where still accurate, generous sharing platters, ribs and wings, cocktailhouse angle, family options, and the character of the historic setting.
- **Positioning line to explore:** “Smoke, steaks & good nights in Fürth.” This is proposed copy, not a claim currently used by the business.

### Reviews and reputation — themes, not verdicts

Tripadvisor currently displays 3.8/5 from 69 reviews and traveller food/interior/menu images. [Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.de/Restaurant_Review-g187306-d2571386-Reviews-Our_Place_Barbecue_Cocktailhouse-Fuerth_Middle_Franconia_Franconia_Bavaria.html) Positive recurring themes include tender, strongly seasoned ribs, generous portions, burgers, wings, coleslaw, cocktails and a well-attended all-you-can-eat offer. Negative recurring themes in individual reviews include service tone, rigid two-hour table windows, reservation handling, noise and perceived value. These are subjective reports, not established facts.

**Recommendation:** Do not use a website to rebut reviews. Instead, make practical expectation-setting visible in the booking handoff: opening hours, kitchen hours, group policy, reservation hold / cancellation rules, seating notes, pre-order requirements and a direct contact path. Add a small “What to know before you come” section. That addresses preventable friction without amplifying criticism.

## 3. Competitor / inspiration takeaways

The closest relevant regional inspiration is Boogie’s Smokehouse & BBQ in Nürnberg. Its site makes its category instantly legible (“Memphis-style / slow-smoked / BBQ”), clearly exposes hours/location, and has useful pages for menu and catering. [Boogie’s home](https://www.boogiesbbq.com/de/) Its menu also explains process and ingredients—22-hour pulled pork, 12-hour brisket, named house sauces—rather than merely listing dishes. [Boogie’s menu](https://www.boogiesbbq.com/boogies_menu_2025_3.pdf)

**Recommendation:** borrow the clarity, not the identity. Our Place should win on its own Fürth personality, direct booking and the social “cocktailhouse + shared barbecue” occasion. A careful market position is: more convivial and historic than a functional smokehouse; more food-led and authentic than a generic bar.

## 4. Recommended website

### Sitemap

1. **Home** (`/`)
2. **Menu** (`/speisekarte`) — filterable structured menu; printable/downloadable version
3. **Reservations** (`/reservieren`) — clear expectations + embedded/linked Resmio
4. **Our Place / Story** (`/unser-place`) — house, team, smoke/fire/cocktail story
5. **Groups & events** (`/gruppen-events`) — only if the owner confirms group/catering offer
6. **Visit** (`/besuch`) — location, opening hours, access, contact, FAQ
7. **Legal** — imprint, privacy, accessibility statement, cookie preferences

Keep take-away as a high-priority external action, not a content page, unless Resmio can support a branded embedded flow reliably.

### Home page sequence

1. **Header:** wordmark, Menu, Story, Visit; primary “Tisch reservieren”; visible menu button on mobile.
2. **Hero:** full-bleed owned image/video still of ribs, fire or a cocktail pour. Eyebrow “Barbecue & Cocktailhouse · Fürth”; H1 “Smoke. Steaks. Good nights.” (German final copy preferred: “Rauch. Ribs. Gute Abende.”); buttons “Tisch reservieren” and “Speisekarte entdecken”. State today’s open/closed status only from managed data.
3. **Proof strip:** “Hickory smoke · house-made favourites · sharing platters” — remove any claim the owner cannot verify.
4. **Signature dishes:** three visual cards: ribs, Smokehouse Burger, a steak/share platter, with brief provenance and menu links.
5. **The occasion:** cocktail + groups / friends block, a soft invitation rather than stock “about us” prose.
6. **Menu teaser:** category tabs, 6 handpicked items, honest price/date metadata, full-menu CTA.
7. **Visit / book:** hours, address, phone, map/directions and booking CTA. Mention pre-order dishes and group questions.
8. **Gallery:** 6–9 real images in a non-autoplay, accessible layout.
9. **Footer:** NAP, legal links, social only after verification.

### Reservations

Use the existing Resmio service to avoid duplicating availability logic. Provide a dedicated page that loads a lightweight booking widget or sends the guest to Resmio in a labelled new tab. Above it, show “For groups / special requests call …” and only confirmed rules. Do not place an iframe above the fold if it delays first content; button-first is a robust fallback. Track CTA clicks with privacy-respecting analytics.

### Menu

Author menu data once (name, category, description, price, allergens, dietary flags, photo, availability, pre-order flag). Render it as semantic HTML and derive a print/PDF representation. Filter chips must work with keyboard and never be the sole way to view items. Include a clear “Prices and availability may change; the restaurant menu applies” note, with a visible “Updated [date]”.

### Visual system and motion

**Recommendation:** charcoal / near-black base, warm bone paper, ember red/orange accents and smoked brass; avoid cliché stars-and-stripes visuals. Typography: a strong condensed display face for headlines plus a highly legible sans for UI/body. Art direction: macro texture, low warm light, ember/smoke cues, editorial crop—not AI food imagery.

Motion should be purposeful: 150–250 ms button/nav feedback; restrained image reveals; a subtle flame/ember texture only if it costs virtually no performance. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`; never auto-play audible video; never make a scrolling or booking action dependent on animation.

### Mobile and accessibility requirements

- Fixed bottom booking button on small screens, but preserve spacing and avoid obscuring content.
- One-thumb paths: menu, book, call, directions. Tap targets at least 44×44 CSS px.
- Semantic landmarks; one H1 per page; logical heading hierarchy; skip link; visible keyboard focus; no hover-only controls.
- Alt text that adds information, captions/transcripts for any meaningful video, contrast at WCAG AA minimum, form labels and useful error messages.
- Do not encode dietary information by colour alone. Publish allergens only when supplied and approved by the restaurant.
- Test on real iPhone/Android widths and keyboard-only navigation before launch.

### SEO and local SEO

- One German-first, location-specific title/H1 per page; natural terms such as “Barbecue Restaurant Fürth”, “Ribs Fürth”, “Steak Fürth” and “Cocktails Fürth”, without keyword stuffing.
- Keep NAP (name, address, phone), current hours, menu categories and booking link consistent across the site and Google Business Profile.
- Implement `Restaurant` JSON-LD with name, address, geo only if accurate, telephone, opening hours, price range only if approved, menu URL, acceptsReservations and sameAs only for confirmed profiles. Add `Menu`/`MenuItem` only from maintained menu data.
- Generate sitemap.xml, robots.txt, canonical URLs, Open Graph images and descriptive social titles. Submit the domain in Search Console after launch.
- Create Google Business Profile posts/photos and respond to reviews operationally; link its booking/menu fields to authoritative URLs.

### Performance and technical guardrails

- Aim for a content-first static site: responsive AVIF/WebP source sets, explicit image dimensions, lazy-load below-the-fold media, preload only the hero image and critical font.
- Use local/system fonts or a self-hosted, subsetted font with `font-display: swap`; minimize third-party scripts.
- Reserve layout dimensions to prevent CLS; do not use a heavy carousel, autoplay background video or a client-side app shell for static content.
- Establish budgets: <200 KB critical JS (ideally far lower), one hero image appropriate to viewport, and monitor Core Web Vitals via real-user data after launch.
- Use a consent platform appropriate to the final analytics stack; do not load non-essential trackers before consent.

## 5. Astro vs Next.js

### Revised recommendation: Next.js for the planned operational features

The earlier Astro recommendation was correct for a brochure/content/booking-handoff site. It should now change to **Next.js** because the requested scope includes a protected staff interface, persistent opening-hours and announcement data, authentication, and a server-side Resend contact-form integration. Next.js App Router has server rendering and server functions available by default, while a static export cannot use server features. [Next.js App Router](https://nextjs.org/docs/app) [Next.js static export](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/single-page-applications)

Use a managed database and authentication provider selected with the owner. The public site can still be cached and statically rendered wherever possible; the staff interface and form submission route need the Next.js runtime. Keep Resend API credentials solely in server environment variables. The contact form should validate server-side, have rate limiting / bot protection, use a transactional confirmation email, and store only the data needed to answer the enquiry. Confirm the privacy/legal basis and retention period with the business before launch.

| Criterion                     | Astro                                                          | Next.js                                                                                                      |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Static marketing/content site | Excellent, static by default                                   | Excellent with static rendering/export                                                                       |
| Default client-JS discipline  | Strong: islands hydrate only what is requested                 | Good, but React/client boundaries need stricter team discipline                                              |
| Content authoring             | Content collections / MDX or a headless CMS                    | CMS-friendly; React ecosystem is very broad                                                                  |
| Dynamic app needs now         | Possible with a server adapter, but a separate design decision | Stronger fit for authenticated staff interface, database-backed hours/announcements and secure form handling |
| Hosting                       | Straightforward static CDN                                     | Static export works on any static host, but server features are unavailable in export mode                   |
| Team choice                   | Best for a lean content-first build                            | **Recommended here:** server-backed features are confirmed                                                   |

Next can output static HTML files with `output: 'export'`, but its server features are not available in that mode. [Next.js static export](https://nextjs.org/docs/app/guides/single-page-applications) **Do not use `output: 'export'` for this build.**

## 6. Staged practical delivery plan

### Stage 0 — technical and content decisions (gate before implementation)

1. Confirm legal business identity, approved logo, contact details, hours and kitchen hours.
2. Confirm menu, prices, allergens/dietary labels, takeaway scope, reservation/group/cancellation rules, all-you-can-eat offer, pre-orders, events/catering and payment/accessibility facilities.
3. Confirm Resmio URLs and whether tracking / embedding is permitted.
4. Supply licensed photography, brand assets and confirmed social handles.
5. Select the stack, hosting, database, authentication provider, domain/DNS access, GDPR-approved analytics/consent approach and the person who owns content updates.
6. Define staff roles (at minimum owner/admin and editor), staff login method, announcement approval/publishing rules, audit needs and the contact-email recipient(s).
7. Confirm the contact-form fields, consent wording, expected response route, email sender domain, retention/deletion policy, and whether staff need enquiry history in the interface or email-only handling.

**Exit criteria:** approved stack and hosting; confirmed operational data; approved form/privacy requirements; Resend sender domain/recipient plan; assets and content owner identified.

### Stage 1 — information architecture, visual direction and data model

- Content model, German copy, photo shot list, wireframe, one approved visual direction.
- Prototype the mobile hero, menu and booking flow first.
- Owner review of every claim, price and dietary indicator.
- Specify database entities: `opening_hours` (including effective dates / exceptions), `announcements` (title, body, status, publish window), `staff_users`, and—only if approved—`contact_enquiries`.

**Exit criteria:** signed-off page structure, visual system, content fields, data model and staff workflows.

### Stage 2 — public-site foundation

- Next.js App Router + TypeScript; CSS tokens; accessible components; Resmio handoff; structured data; legal pages; public pages rendered/cached appropriately.
- Build static pages and keep each component independently testable.

**Exit criteria:** responsive, accessible public experience working with approved placeholder/live data; all booking/order/call/map links tested.

### Stage 3 — staff interface and operational content

- Protected `/staff` interface with authentication and role checks.
- Opening-hours editor: regular weekly hours, kitchen hours, temporary exceptions/closures, preview of public output, and publish action.
- Announcements editor: draft/published status, optional scheduled start/end times, preview, and archive/unpublish control.
- Validate inputs server-side, record editor/time in an audit field where feasible, and expose no staff/admin data publicly.

**Exit criteria:** a non-technical staff member can safely publish a holiday closure and a time-bounded announcement without developer help; public cache invalidation/revalidation has been tested.

### Stage 4 — contact form and Resend

- Public contact form with explicitly approved fields and consent copy.
- Server-side schema validation, rate limiting and bot mitigation; accessible error/success states.
- Send notification to confirmed restaurant recipient(s) via Resend and an acknowledgement to the guest only if approved.
- Configure domain authentication, server-only secrets, failure logging and a safe fallback message. Do not expose an API key to the browser.

**Exit criteria:** successful, invalid, rate-limited and email-provider-failure paths have each been tested in a non-production environment.

### Stage 5 — QA, content migration and launch

- Test current hours, every call/mail/map/booking/order link, form/error states, keyboard, reduced-motion, screen-reader basics, 320 px mobile through desktop, performance, sitemap, canonical URLs, schema validation and consent behaviour.
- Update listings and monitor booking CTA clicks and organic local queries—not vanity page views alone.
- Train staff using a one-page publishing guide and run a launch-day test of a temporary announcement, opening-hours change and contact form.

## 7. Useful coding-agent workflow

**Recommendation:** give the coding agent a compact, versioned source-of-truth folder: `brief.md`, `content/menu.json`, `content/site.json`, `assets/`, `brand/`, `legal/`, and `decisions.md`. Mark every customer-facing claim as approved. Ask the agent to build in thin vertical slices: global shell → home hero/book → menu → visit → legal/SEO → QA. Require screenshots and an accessibility/performance report per milestone.

The current OpenAI guidance recommends explicitly setting task boundaries, follow-through, writing style, delegation where appropriate and proportional verification in an agent prompt. [Official OpenAI model guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/latest-model) Its guidance also notes that skills/instruction files can materially influence an agent, so keep project instructions short, unambiguous and reviewed. For this site, useful reusable skills/workflows are: a research/content-validation checklist, a design-system component skill, an accessibility QA skill, an image-optimisation workflow, and a launch/SEO checklist. Avoid autonomous publishing or changing live reservations/listings without owner confirmation.
